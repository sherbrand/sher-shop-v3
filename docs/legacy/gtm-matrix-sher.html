Shortlisted markets — From you

  2. AS markets. PH, HK, TW, SG. Marketplace-dominant everywhere except HK.

Shortlisted GTM — From you

Five strategies, and the dots they translate to. Ringed on the board.

  • 1. KOL partnership, their photos as paid ad creative. Two dots: KOL and creator partnerships plus paid social ads. One play, two costs. You pay a licence fee for the image, then you pay again for every person who sees it. On the board that is a mid dot at mid commitment married to the biggest dot on the whole board.
  • 2. Get people to tag @sherbrand. Customer content. Low commitment, low CAC, sits in "comparing brands". A tag needs a reason: either the piece is distinctive enough to be worth posting, or you give her one by reposting and featuring. That is the branding question you asked, and the answer is that the garment has to be recognisable in a photo taken by someone else.
  • 3. Organic TikTok and Reels. Organic social. Note what it does not include: atelier footage and founder story are what goes in the videos. Organic social is the shelf, not the product. Both sit low-commitment and low-CAC, unringed for now.
  • 4. SEO and GEO. SEO and AI search, which already covers answer engines. Content marketing is what SEO ranks, so the two run together. Sits in "wants the category", the thinnest and cheapest band on the board.
  • 5. Sell to Chinese-speaking markets. Was not a dot. Now two. Xiaohongshu is ringed: cheap to start and it reaches HK, TW, SG and MY as well as the mainland. CN mainland set is unringed and sits low, which is the argument: mainland commerce is a market you enter, not a channel you add.

Four of your five sit in the cold and middle bands. Nothing on your list reaches "ready to buy", which is the cheapest band on the board and the one nobody is standing in.

    Findings report — From the analysis

    From the matrix

    • Half the board does not care which market you pick. 20 of 42 are marked All: they run from one account, off your own site, and need no local entity anywhere. That is a sharper dividing line than either axis.
    • All eight of the cheapest methods are in that half. Atelier footage, founder story, customer content, review platforms, IG/FB Shop, waitlist and drops, owned list to first order, founder broadcast. Low commitment, low CAC, and none of them needs the geography question settled first.
    • CAC falls as interest rises, and it falls a long way. 59 not looking, 44 wants the category, 42 comparing, 28 ready to buy. Reaching a cold stranger abroad costs about two and a half times what it costs to convert someone already warm.
    • Nothing outbound reaches "ready to buy". Not one method out of nine. You cannot push a person into being ready. She arrives that way, and all you can do is be there. That is the board's strongest argument for building assets over buying reach.
    • "Ready to buy" is the cheapest band and entirely unbuilt. Nine methods, average CAC 28. IG/FB Shop, owned list to first order, waitlist and drops, referral, fit calculator, own site, TikTok Shop abroad, Shopee and Lazada, Xiaohongshu Shop.
    • "Wants the category" is the thinnest band: 7 of 42, one of which is a trade show. Six real doors to a woman who already wants a corset top and has never heard of SHER: paid search, SEO, content marketing, borrowed audiences, partnerships, Xiaohongshu. Demand that already exists is the cheapest demand there is.
    • Big dots sit high, small dots sit low. What takes little to commit to is mostly dear per customer, and what takes a lot up front tends to be cheap per customer after. Paid social is the biggest dot at the top: lowest to commit, dearest to sustain.
    • Orange owns the cold end 10 to 2, blue owns everything warmer 21 to 9, and orange costs about double: 60 average CAC against 31. Getting found the first time is something you go out and do. After that she comes to you. Outbound is the entry fee, not the plan.
    • EN has the most methods, and that is not the same as the best economics. 12 dedicated plus the 20 shared, against 4 for AS and 3 for CN, with 3 more shared across a pair. English markets are reachable through content and search; AS and CN are gated behind marketplaces wanting a local entity. Channel count favours EN. Shipping distance, duties and return cost do not.
    • Both in-app shops sit low, and for the same reason. TikTok Shop and Xiaohongshu Shop want per-country registration, a local entity or agent, a local bank account and local fulfilment. They are two dots rather than one only because the markets differ. Xiaohongshu's content side is the exception, and it stays cheap.

    Three items sit awkwardly on the interest axis: trade shows, wholesale buy-in and consignment act on a business buyer first, not a shopper, so they are placed by where their effect eventually lands. The China figures come from agency guides rather than first-party documentation, and platform rules change.

    From competitors

    Meta Ad Library and site teardowns for the four closest brands in brand-sher.md, read 11 August 2026. Read as evidence about channels, not as brand profiles.

    • Creator-led paid social is proven, and it is your item 1. Stolen Stores has one ad running since 8 January 2026, still live after seven months. The surviving creative is a named creator tagged by handle with every piece listed underneath. An ad that lives seven months is a profitable ad. You already have the screenshot: ss-mooniquelarsen.jpg in your own research folder.
    • Two opposite creative theories are both alive on that same channel. Stolen Stores runs 19-second creator video. Bupbes runs about 100 ads at five to seven seconds with three to five words of copy and no offer at all. Nothing says you must pick one, and nothing says either is the answer.
    • Paid social is not the price of entry. Cherie Attire, the brand your own file calls the closest aspirational match, runs no Meta ads at all and is hiring a social media manager for Instagram and TikTok. Organic social carries a premium brand in this category without paid support.
    • Affiliate is empty ground. Not one of the four has an affiliate or ambassador program. Cherie has a refer-a-friend, which is referral. Either the category does not reward it or nobody has tried, and the difference is worth one afternoon of checking before you rule it in or out.
    • Physical try-on is proven at home, not abroad. Stolen Stores runs a Bangkok showroom, spends money advertising it, and sells made-to-measure. The pop-up dot works, in the market you are ignoring.
    • Physical retail abroad splits in two, and only one half is open to you. Fanci Club carries a stockists page, so the wholesale route works in this category. But wholesale means a foreign buyer bets cash on an unknown brand. Consignment asks them for shelf space instead, which is why it is the door an unknown brand actually gets through. The board now carries both, 25 commitment points apart.
    • Not checked, so not concluded: nobody in this set was tested for SEO, Xiaohongshu presence, or marketplace listings. Those dots stay unevidenced rather than empty.
    • All four are Southeast Asian exporters. Stolen Stores and Cherie are both Bangkok, Cherie at Sukhumvit 101/1. Bupbes and Fanci Club are both Vietnamese. brand-sher.md notes only the first. They carry your duties, your distance and your credibility gap, which is what makes their channel choices worth copying rather than admiring.

    My suggestion

    Opinion, not evidence. Weigh it against the two sections above.

    • Start the eight market-agnostic methods now, before settling geography. They cost little, they compound, and none of them is wasted whichever market you pick. Waiting on the geography decision to start them is the one clear mistake available to you.
    • Reconsider HK at the top of your market list. A free port, so no duty surprise. Days from your warehouse rather than weeks. English and Chinese both work. Instagram-native and Xiaohongshu-active, so two of your six shortlisted methods point straight at it. And you flagged it yourself as the one AS market that is not marketplace-dominant, which means no local entity to sell there.
    • Set refunds and first market together. They are one decision. Exchange and store credit is what all four competitors run and it survives in HK, TW and SG where the parcel is days away. In EN markets the same policy is a gate, not a drag.
    • Switch duties to DDP. Today the buyer meets a customs bill she did not agree to, and refusing the parcel means no refund. A surprise charge at the door is the most common cause of refused parcels and chargebacks. It costs margin instead of costing the customer.
    • Buy the shipping speed back rather than explaining it. Stolen Stores gives free worldwide express over $299 and lets the threshold pay for it. Nine to seventeen days stated plainly on the product page is the cheaper alternative, and the weaker one.
    • Whatever you settle on for payment, assume card is not enough. Add Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal for EN, and the local options for AS before you sell there.
